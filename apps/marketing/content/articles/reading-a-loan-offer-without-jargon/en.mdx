## Loan offers are designed to look good

Banks and lenders want you to say yes. The offer letter highlights the monthly payment and the speed of approval. The terms that actually determine what you pay – the total cost, the penalties, the conditions – are buried in fine print and technical language.

You do not need a finance degree to read a loan offer properly. You just need to know which numbers matter and which words to watch for.

## The numbers that actually matter

### The interest rate vs. the effective rate

The **nominal interest rate** is the basic rate the lender charges. But it is not the whole cost. The **effective interest rate** (sometimes called the EIR, APR or "efektivna kamatna stopa" / "THM") includes fees, insurance and other charges rolled into the cost of borrowing.

Always compare effective rates, not nominal ones. Two loans with the same nominal rate can have very different total costs once fees are included.

### The total amount repayable

This is the number most people skip. It tells you exactly how much you will pay back in total – the principal (what you borrowed) plus all interest and fees. A lower monthly payment spread over a longer term often means you pay significantly more in total.

### The monthly payment

This matters for your budget, but it is not the best way to compare offers. A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises the total cost. Know both numbers before you decide.

<Callout kind="note">
If a lender emphasises how low the monthly payment is without clearly showing the total repayable amount or the effective rate, treat that as a signal to read the fine print more carefully.
</Callout>

## The terms to understand

### Fixed vs. variable rate

- **Fixed rate:** the interest rate stays the same for the life of the loan (or for a set period). Your payments are predictable.
- **Variable rate:** the rate changes based on a reference rate (like EURIBOR or the central bank rate). Your payments can go up or down.

Variable rates often start lower, but you carry the risk of increases. If you choose a variable rate, ask: what is the maximum my payment could reach?

### Early repayment

Can you pay the loan off early, and if so, is there a fee? Early repayment penalties vary widely. Some lenders charge a percentage of the remaining balance; others charge nothing. If there is a chance you might pay the loan off ahead of schedule, this clause matters.

### Collateral and guarantees

Some loans require collateral (property, a car, a savings account) or a guarantor. Understand exactly what is at stake if you cannot repay. If you are asked to sign as a guarantor for someone else, know that you are taking on their debt if they default.

### Currency

In Serbia, some loans are denominated in euros or indexed to the euro. In Hungary, forint-denominated loans are standard now, but older foreign-currency loans caused serious problems in the past. Make sure you understand what currency your repayment is calculated in and what happens if the exchange rate moves.

## Before you sign

### Compare at least three offers

Do not accept the first offer your bank presents. Get written offers from at least three lenders and compare them side by side: effective rate, total repayable, monthly payment, early repayment terms, and any mandatory insurance.

### Ask the awkward questions

- What happens if I miss a payment?
- Can the rate change, and if so, by how much?
- Are there any fees not included in the effective rate?
- What is the total I will pay over the life of the loan?

A good lender answers these clearly. If they deflect or rush you, slow down.

### Take the offer home

You do not have to sign on the spot. A legitimate lender will give you time to review the offer, ask questions and seek advice. In both Serbia and Hungary, you have a legal right to review the terms before committing.
