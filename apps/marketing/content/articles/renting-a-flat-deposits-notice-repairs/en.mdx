## Renting should not feel like guesswork

Finding a flat is stressful enough. Understanding what you owe, what the landlord owes, and what happens when things go wrong should not add to the pressure.

Whether you rent in Serbia or Hungary, the basics are similar: you pay a deposit, you agree to a notice period, and someone is responsible for repairs. The details, however, vary -- and those details matter when a dispute arises.

This guide covers the practical points every tenant should understand.

## The deposit

A deposit (sometimes called a security deposit or "kapar" / "kaució") is money you pay upfront, usually before moving in. The landlord holds it as a guarantee against damage or unpaid rent.

### What to check

- **How much?** One to two months' rent is standard. Anything more than that is worth questioning.
- **Is it documented?** Get a receipt. If the contract does not mention the deposit amount, add it.
- **Under what conditions is it returned?** The contract should state clearly when and how you get your deposit back after you leave.
- **What can the landlord deduct?** Normal wear and tear -- scuff marks on walls, minor carpet wear -- is usually not deductible. Damage beyond normal use may be.

<Callout kind="tip">
Take photos of the flat on the day you move in, including any existing damage. Email them to yourself and the landlord so you have a dated record. This simple step prevents most deposit disputes.
</Callout>

### Common problems

The most frequent dispute is about what counts as "damage." A written inventory with photos, signed by both sides, makes this much simpler to resolve.

## The notice period

The notice period is how much time you or the landlord must give before ending the tenancy.

### Fixed-term vs. indefinite leases

- **Fixed-term:** The contract runs for a set period (often 12 months). You usually cannot leave early without penalty unless the contract allows it. The landlord also cannot ask you to leave before the end date without cause.
- **Indefinite:** Either side can end the tenancy by giving notice. The notice period is set by the contract or by law (often 30 to 90 days).

### What to check

- How long is the notice period for each side?
- Must notice be given in writing?
- Are there penalties for leaving early on a fixed-term lease?
- What counts as a valid reason for the landlord to end the contract early?

## Repairs and maintenance

This is where many tenants and landlords disagree, because the line between "your responsibility" and "their responsibility" is not always drawn clearly.

### The general rule

- **Landlord:** responsible for structural repairs, plumbing, heating systems, electrical wiring, and anything that was broken or worn out before you moved in.
- **Tenant:** responsible for minor maintenance (replacing light bulbs, keeping the flat clean, not blocking drains) and for repairing damage you caused.

### What to check in the contract

- Does the contract specify who pays for appliance repairs?
- Is there a clause about "returning the flat in its original condition"? If so, what does that mean in practice?
- How do you report a problem, and how quickly must the landlord respond?

### When something breaks

Report it in writing -- a message or email, not just a phone call. Describe the problem, attach a photo if relevant, and keep a copy. If the landlord does not respond, follow up in writing again. A paper trail matters if the issue escalates.

## If things go wrong

Most tenancy disputes can be resolved with a calm conversation and a clear contract. But when they cannot, you have options:

- **Mediation.** Some cities offer free or low-cost mediation services for tenant-landlord disputes.
- **Legal advice.** A short consultation with a lawyer can clarify your rights and your best next step.
- **Tenant associations.** In Hungary, tenant advocacy groups can advise you on your rights.
