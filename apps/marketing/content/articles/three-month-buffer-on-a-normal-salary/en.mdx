## Why three months?

Three months of living expenses -- not income, just expenses -- is enough to cover most common financial emergencies: a broken appliance, an unexpected medical bill, a gap between jobs. It gives you breathing room so that a single bad surprise does not spiral into debt.

You do not need a high salary to build this buffer. You need a realistic plan and enough patience to stick with it.

## Step 1: Find out what you actually spend

Before you can save, you need to know where your money goes. Track your spending for one full month. Every coffee, every bill, every subscription.

Sort your spending into three groups:

- **Fixed costs:** rent, utilities, insurance, loan payments, transport pass. These are hard to change quickly.
- **Regular but flexible:** groceries, phone plan, streaming services, gym membership. You can adjust these.
- **Discretionary:** eating out, clothes, entertainment, impulse purchases. These are the easiest to reduce.

Add up the fixed and flexible costs. That total is roughly what you need per month to get by. Multiply by three -- that is your target.

<Callout kind="tip">
Most people overestimate their fixed costs and underestimate their discretionary spending. Tracking for just one month often reveals small leaks -- subscriptions you forgot about, delivery fees that add up, daily habits that cost more than you thought.
</Callout>

## Step 2: Set a monthly savings amount

Look at your discretionary spending. How much could you realistically redirect to savings each month without making yourself miserable?

Be honest. If you set the number too high, you will give up in two months. A smaller amount that you stick to is worth more than an ambitious target you abandon.

For example: if your monthly expenses are 80,000 RSD (or 250,000 HUF), your target is 240,000 RSD (or 750,000 HUF). Saving 15,000 RSD a month gets you there in 16 months. That is not fast, but it is real.

## Step 3: Automate it

Set up an automatic transfer on payday. The money moves to a separate account before you see it in your spending account. This single step makes the biggest difference, because it removes the daily decision to save.

Use a savings account at your bank -- it does not need to be anything fancy. The goal is separation, not interest.

## Step 4: Protect the buffer

Your emergency fund is for emergencies, not opportunities. A discounted holiday is not an emergency. A broken boiler in January is.

Set a simple rule for yourself: you only touch this money when the expense is unexpected, necessary and urgent. If it does not meet all three criteria, it waits.

## Step 5: Rebuild after you use it

If you do need to dip into the fund, start rebuilding it as soon as you can. You already know the routine -- the same automatic transfer, the same amount. The habit is the hard part, and you have already built it.

## Common questions

**What if I have debt?** If your debt carries high interest (credit cards, payday loans), paying that down first usually makes more sense. A small emergency fund of one month's expenses alongside debt repayment is a reasonable compromise.

**What if my income is irregular?** Save a percentage instead of a fixed amount. Even 5 percent of each payment adds up. The automatic transfer still works -- just set it for the day after your most reliable income arrives.

**Does it have to be exactly three months?** No. One month is better than nothing. Two months is better than one. The point is progress, not perfection.
