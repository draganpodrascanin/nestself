## Why the contract matters more than the offer letter

The offer letter tells you the good news: your title, your salary, your start date. The employment contract is where the obligations live -- yours and your employer's. It is a legally binding document, and once you sign it, changing the terms requires both sides to agree.

Whether you are starting a new job in Serbia or Hungary, or reviewing a contract you signed years ago, knowing what to look for protects you.

## The basics: make sure they are clear

These should be spelled out in plain language:

- **Your job title and description.** What are you expected to do? Vague descriptions like "other duties as assigned" can be stretched later. The more specific, the better.
- **Working hours.** How many hours per week? Is overtime expected, and how is it compensated? Both Serbian and Hungarian law set limits on working hours, but your contract may define a different arrangement within those limits.
- **Salary and payment schedule.** The gross amount, the net amount (or how to calculate it), when you are paid, and whether bonuses or commissions are included.
- **Start date and contract type.** Is this a permanent (indefinite) contract or a fixed-term one? If fixed-term, when does it end, and under what conditions can it be renewed?

## Probation period

Most contracts include a probation period -- typically one to six months. During this time, either side can end the employment with shorter notice. Check:

- How long is the probation?
- What happens at the end? Is confirmation automatic, or does the employer need to approve it?
- What is the notice period during probation?

## Notice periods and termination

This is the section people most often wish they had read more carefully.

- **Your notice period.** How much advance notice must you give if you want to leave? Common periods range from 15 days to three months.
- **The employer's notice period.** How much notice must they give you? In both Serbia and Hungary, the law sets minimum notice periods that the contract cannot reduce.
- **Grounds for termination.** Under what circumstances can the employer end the contract? Are there any restrictive clauses?

## Non-compete and confidentiality clauses

Some contracts include a clause that limits where you can work after you leave. Check:

- **How long does it last?** Six months? A year? Two years?
- **How broad is it?** Does it cover your entire industry or just direct competitors?
- **Is there compensation?** In many jurisdictions, a non-compete clause must come with financial compensation during the restricted period. If yours does not, it may not be enforceable.

Confidentiality clauses are standard and usually reasonable. But read them -- make sure you understand what counts as confidential information.

## Benefits and leave

- **Annual leave.** The legal minimum differs between Serbia and Hungary, but your contract may offer more. Check the number of days and how they accrue.
- **Sick leave.** How is it handled and compensated?
- **Other benefits.** Health insurance, meal allowances, transport, training budgets, and -- where your employer's plan includes it -- wellbeing support such as NestSelf.

## What to do if something is unclear

Ask. Before you sign, you have every right to ask questions, request changes, or take a day to review the document. A reasonable employer expects this.

If you are unsure about a specific clause, a short conversation with a lawyer can save you from problems later. You do not need to hire someone for a full review -- even a 30-minute consultation can clarify the points that matter most.
