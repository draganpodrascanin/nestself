## 2. Scope decisions

**D-CW-01 (ruled). One epic, `NS-CALLS-01 "Consultations and webinars on Amazon Chime SDK"`, with four milestones.** It delivers the real-time layer for three formats: private 1:1 consultations (expert and employee), webinars (one or two presenters, a moderator, attendees who listen and watch, write to the hosts and may be given the floor by a host) and small group workshops (up to 25 participants, everyone on video). Milestone order: M0 foundation and spikes; M1 consultations with the end-of-call follow-up (D-CW-37); M2 webinars live up to 250 attendees, always recorded, with the replay handoff; M3 webinar scale beyond 250 (replication), certificates and the load test; M4 mobile join, workshops and replay captions. Why one epic: the three formats share the provider adapter, the session and participant model, the pre-join and in-call client, the event ingestion and the diagnostics. Alternative rejected: two epics (calls, webinars) would duplicate M0 and M1 client work.

**D-CW-02 (ruled). This epic owns a minimal appointment spine, not the booking engine.** Nothing about appointments exists in the code today (survey section 2). The epic adds an `appointments` table and module with exactly what a call needs: participants, service category label, schedule, status, a nullable `funding_ref`, and transitions `proposed`, `awaiting_approval`, `confirmed`, `completed`, `canceled`, `declined`, `no_show`. Appointments are created by an expert or a service coordinator for a named employee (actions `appointment.propose`, `bookings.coordinate`), as a follow-up proposed at the end of a call (D-CW-37) and by the seed. Availability, slots, holds, direct booking, matching, funding reservation, insurer units, approvals, rescheduling fees and cancellation terms (F2 to F6, F9 steps 1 to 4) are a separate epic, `NS-BOOKING-01`, which will replace the creation path and keep the spine. The seam is the `Appointment` contract in `packages/contracts/src/appointments.ts`; the booking epic may add columns but must not change the fields the call layer reads (`id`, `orgId`, `expertUserId`, `employeeUserId`, `startsAt`, `endsAt`, `status`, `serviceCategory`, `timeZone`).

**D-CW-03 (ruled). Webinar occurrences are owned here; webinar replays are owned by the CMS.** This epic owns the `webinars` table (occurrence, presenters, moderator, audience policy, capacity, chat policy, status), registration, reminders, the live session, the chat and questions, polls, hand-raise and the floor, attendance and the recording pipeline up to a finished MP4 in the recordings bucket (every webinar is recorded, D-CW-34). The handoff creates a draft content item through the CMS media module's interface (NS-CONTENT-01 chapters 6, 17 to 19); review, publication, audience entitlement and protected delivery of the replay are the CMS epic's. Until the CMS media module exists, the handoff writes the object and a `webinar_recordings` row with `handoff_status = 'pending'`; a one-line adapter is added when the CMS interface lands.

**D-CW-04 (ruled). Out of scope for the whole epic.** Phone dial-in and SMS (INFRA-R-21 forbids SMS at launch), professional notes of any kind (D-22), insurer or employer settlement from delivery status (the booking and finance epics consume `call_outcomes`), employer reports beyond the guarantee that no employer-facing endpoint can list participants, live captions (D-CW-23), webinars above 1,000 attendees (documented path only, D-CW-14), native mobile Chime SDK modules (D-CW-24, F-CW-05), chat as a messaging channel in consultations (D-CW-27), extending a running session (F-CW-06, replaced by the follow-up of D-CW-37), attendee cameras in webinars (D-CW-35), a Hungarian launch (D-CW-38).

## 3. Provider and lifecycle

**D-CW-05 (settled, D-22). Provider adapter.** `apps/api/src/video/` defines the abstract class `VideoProvider` (injection token `VIDEO_PROVIDER`) with `createSession`, `createParticipant`, `updateParticipantCapabilities`, `removeParticipant`, `endSession`, `startRecording`, `stopRecording`, `describeSession`. `ChimeVideoProvider` wraps `@aws-sdk/client-chime-sdk-meetings` and `@aws-sdk/client-chime-sdk-media-pipelines`; `FakeVideoProvider` returns deterministic stubs and emits the same domain events locally. `VIDEO_PROVIDER=none` selects the fake (the default in development and in every test that is not the nightly real-provider smoke test). Only the `chime-sdk-meetings` namespace is used; the legacy `chime` namespace is deprecated and us-east-1 only (CORE-37, CORE-39).

**D-CW-06 (settled). Regions.** Control region, media region and media-pipeline region are all `eu-central-1`, hard-coded in the adapter and validated by the env schema (`CHIME_REGION` must equal `eu-central-1`; any other value fails start-up). The adapter never uses nearest-region selection. Meeting metadata, media, pipelines and events then stay in Frankfurt (CORE-09 to CORE-12, LAW-05). No claim of "nothing leaves the EU" is made in product copy; AWS does not guarantee it for billing telemetry (LAW-06), and audio is mixed server-side, so the product says "encrypted in transit and at rest", never "end-to-end encrypted" (LAW-07).

**D-CW-07 (ruled). Create the meeting at first join, not at booking.** A Chime meeting auto-ends after 5 minutes without attendees and after 24 hours (CORE-16, OPS-03), so a meeting created at booking would be gone before the session. The first participant who calls `POST /appointments/:id/join` inside the window triggers `CreateMeetingWithAttendees` (meeting plus their attendee in one call, OPS-05); the second participant triggers `CreateAttendee`. A `KeyValueStore.set(key, value, { onlyIfAbsent: true, ttlSeconds })` lock on `call:{sessionId}:create` serialises concurrent first joins; the loser waits for the winner's `call_sessions.provider_meeting_id` (poll up to 3 seconds) and then creates its attendee. `ClientRequestToken` is the `call_sessions.id`; `ExternalMeetingId` is also the `call_sessions.id`. Whether a repeated `ClientRequestToken` returns the same meeting is undocumented (OPS-01), so the lock is the idempotency mechanism and the token is a second line of defence. **SPIKE-CW-01** measures the real behaviour in the dev account.

**D-CW-08 (ruled). Rejoin reuses the attendee; credentials live in Redis for the window.** Join tokens have no clock TTL (CORE-17, OPS-06) and reusing one from a new device hands the session over (`AttendeeAuthorized`, OPS-08). The adapter stores `{ meeting, attendee }` under `call:{sessionId}:participant:{participantId}` with TTL until `endsAt + 15 minutes`; a repeated join returns the cached credentials. The join token is never written to PostgreSQL, never logged, never put in an audit `details` object (the audit writer's forbidden-key list already blocks `token`), and the join response carries `Cache-Control: no-store`. `DeleteAttendee` is called only when access is revoked (appointment cancelled while live, participant removed by a moderator). **SPIKE-CW-02** checks `CreateAttendee` with a repeated `ExternalUserId` (OPS open question 2).

**D-CW-09 (ruled). Explicit end and a safety net.** The expert's "end for everyone" and the moderator's "end webinar" call `DeleteMeeting` immediately (OPS-04). A BullMQ delayed job `calls.cleanup` scheduled at `endsAt + 15 minutes` deletes the meeting if the provider still reports it, and a 5-minute sweep (`calls.reconcile`) catches jobs lost to a worker restart. The in-call client shows "session time has ended" at `endsAt`, a warning at `endsAt + 10 minutes`, and the room closes at `endsAt + 15 minutes`. No session extension exists in this epic (F-CW-06, answered); instead the post-call screens let both parties schedule a follow-up in a few minutes without the video running (D-CW-37). Recording attendees keep a meeting alive (OPS-42), so the cleanup job runs regardless of attendee count.

**D-CW-10 (ruled). Meeting features per format.**

| Format | `Audio.EchoReduction` | `Video.MaxResolution` | `Content.MaxResolution` | `Attendee.MaxCount` | Attendee capabilities |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Consultation | `AVAILABLE` | `HD` (720p) | `FHD` | 4 | both `SendReceive` on audio and video; content `SendReceive` for the expert, `Receive` for the employee |
| Webinar (single meeting) | `AVAILABLE` | `HD` | `FHD` | 250 | presenters and moderator `SendReceive` on all; attendees `Receive` on audio, video and content; an attendee given the floor becomes `SendReceive` on audio only, video stays `Receive` (D-CW-35) |
| Workshop | `AVAILABLE` | `HD` | `FHD` | 30 | all `SendReceive`; content `SendReceive` for facilitator, `Receive` for others until promoted |

`FHD` video would cap the meeting at 25 attendees (CORE-06) and is never requested. `MaxCount` for consultations is 4, not 2, to leave room for a recording attendee and a support join in a later phase without a contract change. Echo reduction is a meeting-level feature with no published price (COST-09); it is enabled because many employees will use laptop speakers (CORE-26). **SPIKE-CW-03** confirms whether a media-pipeline attendee counts against `MaxCount`.

**D-CW-11 (ruled). Identities handed to the provider are opaque.** `ExternalUserId` is the `call_participants.id` (a random id created per session and participant), never the user id, name or email (LAW-09). `TenantIds` carries the organisation id for consultations and workshops and the webinar id for webinars (OPS-37); AppKeys are not configured in this epic. Tags: `stage`, `format`, `orgId` only.

**D-CW-12 (ruled). Event ingestion.** Every `CreateMeeting` passes `NotificationsConfiguration.SqsQueueArn` pointing at the stack's `chime-events` queue (lower latency than EventBridge, OPS-14); an EventBridge rule on `source = aws.chime` to the same queue is the backstop, so every event arrives at least once and sometimes twice. The worker process polls the queue (`@aws-sdk/client-sqs`, long polling, visibility timeout 60 seconds, dead-letter queue after 5 receives) and writes each event to `call_events` with a unique key `(provider_meeting_id, event_type, provider_attendee_id, occurred_at)`; duplicates are dropped before any state change. Events are applied out of order safely: `AttendeeJoined` sets `first_joined_at` only if null, `AttendeeLeft` and `AttendeeDropped` set `last_left_at` to the max, `MeetingEnded` closes the session once (OPS-12, OPS-15). Attendee-minutes are derived from the event table, not from client reports. Locally and in tests, `FakeVideoProvider` emits the same events to the same handler through an in-process queue.

**D-CW-13 (ruled). Client observability.** The web client posts the Chime SDK client events `meetingStartSucceeded`, `meetingStartFailed`, `meetingFailed`, `meetingReconnected`, `audioInputFailed`, `videoInputFailed`, `signalingDropped` (OPS-19, OPS-20) plus a 30-second aggregate of `getStats()` (round-trip time, jitter, packet loss, available outgoing bitrate, UX-36, UX-39) to `POST /calls/:sessionId/client-events`. Rows go to `call_client_events` (retention 90 days) and carry only metrics, device error names, browser and OS; never device labels, never names, never content. Chime's own event ingestion endpoint (`EventIngestionUrl`) is not used, so no client telemetry goes to a third endpoint. Alerts: join failure rate above 5 percent over 15 minutes, p95 `meetingStartDurationMs` above 5 seconds, any `meetingFailed` burst (OPS-44).

## 4. Webinars

**D-CW-14 (ruled). Delivery model by audience size.** Up to 250 attendees: one meeting with receive-only attendees (WEB-01 to WEB-05). 251 to 1,000: media replication, one primary (presenters) plus replicas created with `PrimaryMeetingId`; replica attendees see only the primary's media and never each other (WEB-07 to WEB-09), cost plus $0.0085 per replica-session-minute (WEB-20). Above 1,000 or unpredictable: presenter meeting plus media live connector to Amazon IVS (WEB-14, WEB-17, WEB-21), with Q&A on Socket.IO because IVS viewers are not attendees. M2 implements the single meeting; replication is M3 behind the same adapter (`createSession({ format: 'webinar', capacity })` decides), pulled forward from M4 because the founder expects 200 to 300 attendees in the first year and accepts a ceiling only for technical reasons (F-CW-02, D-CW-36); the IVS path is documented, not built. Registration is capped at the delivery model's capacity, and the occurrence form refuses a capacity above 250 until M3 lands.

**D-CW-15 (ruled). Attendee anonymity is enforced server-side, not by the client.** Attendees get receive-only capabilities, so they cannot send audio or video (WEB-01). The roster, counts and names come from NestSelf's database, never from `ListAttendees` on the client: attendees receive no roster at all, presenters receive a count, the moderator sees registered names for promotion and follow-up (UX-25, UX-31, UX-48). Attendees never send video, in any milestone (D-CW-35). Messages and questions from attendees are anonymous towards the audience by default; an attendee can attach their first name per message. Hand-raise shows the moderator the registration name; the presenter sees "a participant asked to speak". Giving the floor is a host action: `UpdateAttendeeCapabilities` sets audio to `SendReceive` and leaves video at `Receive` (WEB-03); the attendee is told "you can speak now, unmute to talk" and stays muted until they unmute; the host can take the floor back at any time, which reverses the capability. The audio dropout reported for promoted attendees in replica meetings (WEB-39) does not apply to single meetings; it becomes **SPIKE-CW-06** in M3 when replication is built.

**D-CW-16 (ruled). Interaction runs on Socket.IO and PostgreSQL, not on Chime data messages or Chime messaging.** The webinar chat (D-CW-35), hand-raise, polls, "running late" and "I cannot hear you" signals use rooms `call:{sessionId}`, `webinar:{webinarId}` and `webinar:{webinarId}:moderation` on the existing gateway, each subscription authorised with `authorize()` (`consultation.join`, `event.register`, `event.moderate` on the session or webinar resource). Messages, poll definitions and aggregate poll results persist in PostgreSQL; individual votes are stored without the voter id (WEB-35 to WEB-37 for the rejected alternatives). Chime data messages are reserved for future ephemeral in-meeting signals and are not used in this epic.

**D-CW-17 (ruled). Audience policy and registration.** A webinar's audience is `all_eligible` (every active member of every client organisation whose plan includes webinars), one or more organisations, or one or more organisational units of one organisation. Eligibility is evaluated at registration and again at join. Registration needs `event.register` on the webinar resource; the occurrence is visible only to eligible members. Capacity is enforced at registration with `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE` on the occurrence row (SYS-02). Reminders go at 24 hours and 1 hour before start (D-CW-26). Join windows: presenters and the moderator may join from 30 minutes before the scheduled start (they need a device check and a dry run), attendees from 15 minutes before; the lobby shows the countdown until the moderator starts the webinar; the room closes at the scheduled end plus 15 minutes unless the moderator ends it earlier. Employers never receive a registration or attendance list; `GET /ops/webinars/:id/attendance` serves the moderator and NestSelf operations only and is covered by the default-deny test and an authorisation golden case.

**D-CW-18 (settled by the founder, F-CW-01). Every webinar is recorded, presenter-only by construction.** There is no recording policy on an occurrence: recording starts when the moderator starts the webinar and stops when it ends (the moderator can stop it earlier). The worker creates a media capture pipeline on the meeting with `SelectedVideoStreams` set to the presenter participants' attendee ids and `CompositedVideo` layout `PresenterOnly` at HD, audio muxed with the composited video, individual video and content artifacts disabled (WEB-23 to WEB-26). Attendees are receive-only, so no attendee video can exist in the capture; an attendee given the floor is told, before they unmute, that their voice is part of the recording. A persistent "REC" indicator is shown to everyone (UX-45), and the registration page, the confirmation email and the lobby say that the webinar is recorded and that only the presenters are on the recording (F8 step 3). On stop, the worker runs a concatenation pipeline into one MP4 (WEB-29) and then the CMS handoff (D-CW-03). The recordings bucket is in eu-central-1 with SSE-KMS on the platform key, block-public-access, a bucket policy for `mediapipelines.chime.amazonaws.com` with `aws:SourceAccount` and `aws:SourceArn` conditions (WEB-27), and a lifecycle rule that expires raw segments after 30 days. **SPIKE-CW-04** verifies that `SelectedVideoStreams` and `CompositedVideo` combine as intended (WEB open question 3). The default media-pipeline quota in eu-central-1 is 10 concurrent pipelines (WEB-41); a quota increase is requested before the first month with more than 5 recorded webinars.

**D-CW-19 (settled by the founder, F-CW-01). Consultations and workshops are not recorded for now.** No media-pipeline permission is granted to the API role, only to the worker role for webinars; `POST /appointments/:id/recording/*` does not exist. The dual-consent data model (`call_consents` with per-participant, per-session, timestamped, revocable rows, LAW-28, UX-08) is specified in the guidelines so that a later epic can enable consultation recording after the DPIA (LAW-12, LAW-29) if the founder decides so.

## 5. Client

**D-CW-20 (ruled). One web client package, `@nestself/video`, built on `amazon-chime-sdk-js` directly.** `packages/video` holds a framework-agnostic `CallSessionController` (device management, join, reconnect, capability changes, metrics, Chime SDK observers), React hooks (`useCallSession`, `useDevices`, `useConnectionQuality`, `useVideoTiles`, `useWebinarStage`) and the feature components (`PreJoinCheck`, `CallStage`, `ControlBar`, `ConnectionBadge`, `SessionTimer`, `FollowUpCard`, `WebinarStage`, `ChatPanel`, `PollPanel`, `ModeratorQueue`, `HandRaiseButton`, `CrisisBlock`) composed from `@nestself/ui` primitives and tokens. The AWS React component library (`amazon-chime-sdk-component-library-react`) is not used: its package manifest declares styled-components and it carries its own theme, which cannot satisfy the colour and motion rules without a fork (CORE-22 covers only the library's maintenance status and React 19 support; the dependency observation comes from the package itself). `amazon-chime-sdk-js` 3.32.x is the pin (CORE-21). Background blur, Voice Focus and echo reduction need `*.sdkassets.chime.aws` in the content security policy and the client networks need UDP 3478 and TCP 443 to `*.chime.aws` (CORE-30); both go in the client-company network note and the support runbook.

**D-CW-21 (ruled). Pre-join is mandatory and is a custom component.** Before any join, the client runs camera, microphone, speaker and network checks with the Chime SDK's `MeetingReadinessChecker` (OPS-30) and a self-view preview, offers "join with camera off", background blur (opt-in, remembered per device in `localStorage`), device selection and browser-specific permission help (UX-01 to UX-04, UX-11). After the preview, camera and microphone are on by default for consultation and workshop participants of every role; the employee's "join with camera off" choice is remembered per device; webinar attendees have no camera or microphone by construction (D-CW-15). The consultation waiting state shows the expert's first name, photo and credentials, the appointment time, session tips, a "running late" message if the expert sent one, and the late-join and closed-window states of F9 (UX-05 to UX-07). The webinar lobby shows title, presenter, start time, a countdown, the sentence "this webinar is recorded; only the presenters are on the recording" and the sentence "your camera stays off; you can write to the hosts and raise your hand to ask for the floor" (UX-28, D-CW-34, D-CW-35).

**D-CW-22 (ruled). In-call behaviour.** Consultations use speaker view with a movable, hideable self-view (UX-10); the control bar has mute, camera, blur, device switch, fullscreen, "I cannot hear you" quick signal, leave, and for the expert "end for everyone"; a connection badge with three states; degraded mode first lowers resolution, then pauses outgoing video with a banner and resumes when quality recovers (UX-12, UX-38); reconnection shows "connection lost, reconnecting" for up to 2 minutes (the SDK's default) and then offers rejoin (CORE-17, UX-13, UX-34); a session timer with a dismissable "5 minutes remaining" cue (UX-15) that also reminds the expert that a follow-up can be proposed on the post-call screen (D-CW-37); the crisis block (Serbian hotlines, the platform disclaimer, and for the expert the emergency guidance; Hungarian numbers are added only with a Hungarian launch, D-CW-38) is one tap away at all times and is the same `CrisisBlock` the wellbeing check-ins use (UX-17). All controls are keyboard-operable with visible focus, state changes are announced through the existing `LiveRegion`, and every panel, sheet and banner follows the Motion rule including reduced motion (UX-19, UX-42). At 390 px the remote video fills the screen, the self-view floats, the control bar has 44 px targets and secondary controls collapse into an overflow menu (UX-20).

**D-CW-23 (ruled). Captions.** No live captions in this epic: `StartMeetingTranscription` does not accept Serbian or Hungarian (WEB-31, WEB-34), and a custom Transcribe streaming path through a media stream pipeline is an M4 candidate at the earliest. Replay captions for webinars (Transcribe batch, which supports `sr-RS` and `hu-HU`, WEB-32, converted to WebVTT and reviewed by a person before publication) are an M4 item on the CMS handoff; Serbian is the first language, Hungarian follows for webinars held in Hungarian (D-CW-38). The accessibility statement for webinars says live captions are not yet available and replays are captioned (LAW-33).

**D-CW-24 (ruled). Mobile join in the first version is the web client inside the mobile app.** There is no maintained React Native or Expo module for the Chime SDK (CORE-34, CORE-35). The employee app opens a dedicated call route of the workspace web app (`/call/:appointmentId`) in an in-app browser view; WKWebView supports audio and video with the JS SDK (CORE-23, CORE-36). Because the mobile app has bearer tokens and no web session, the app first calls `POST /appointments/:id/call-ticket` (bearer), receives a 30-second, single-use ticket (same pattern as `POST /auth/ws-ticket`), and the web route exchanges it at `POST /appointments/:id/join` for credentials. Screen share is unavailable on mobile, which is acceptable for employees. Native modules through the Expo Modules API, CallKit integration and background audio are a later epic if the pilot shows the need (F-CW-05). **SPIKE-CW-05** runs the web route in the "NestSelf iPhone" simulator and on one physical Android device.

## 6. Data, privacy and operations

**D-CW-25 (ruled). Tables and their tenancy.** `appointments`, `appointment_approvals` (D-CW-37), `call_sessions`, `call_participants`, `call_events`, `call_client_events`, `call_outcomes`, `call_feedback`, `call_consents` (schema only, D-CW-19), `call_messages` (M4), `webinars`, `webinar_hosts`, `webinar_registrations`, `webinar_messages` (chat and questions, D-CW-35), `webinar_polls`, `webinar_poll_options`, `webinar_poll_results`, `webinar_recordings`, `webinar_certificates`. Appointment-side tables carry `org_id` (the employee's organisation) and are under forced RLS with `tenantPredicate`; expert access to appointments in organisations they are assigned to goes through the same org scope the operations routes use for assigned clients (phase 2 Task 1 pattern) plus the participant check in `authorize()`. Webinar tables for `all_eligible` occurrences carry `org_id = null` and a policy that allows the platform scope; organisation-scoped occurrences carry the organisation id. The guidelines spell out every policy. `call_sessions` carries both the scheduled window (`starts_at`, `ends_at`, copied from the appointment or occurrence) and the actual `started_at` and `ended_at`. PostgreSQL enum values follow the repository convention and are upper case (`CONFIRMED`, `NO_SHOW_EMPLOYEE`); the lower-case spellings in this record are the contract and API spellings, mapped once in the repository layer. `call_outcomes` holds the delivery status (`completed`, `no_show_employee`, `no_show_expert`, `technical_failure`, `canceled`) and connection metadata only; a free-text field does not exist (no professional notes, D-22, EXP-07). `call_feedback` holds a 1 to 5 score and an optional comment visible to NestSelf operations in aggregate and never to the employer (UX-22).

**D-CW-26 (ruled). Notifications are neutral.** Email reminders at 24 hours and 1 hour before a consultation or webinar, and the registration confirmation, use the subject "Your NestSelf session" or "Your NestSelf webinar", carry no service category, and attach an ICS file with `CLASS:PRIVATE`, a neutral `SUMMARY`, the authenticated join link in `DESCRIPTION` and `X-MICROSOFT-CDO-BUSYSTATUS:BUSY` (UX-24, UX-32). The ICS generator is a small in-house module with tests (RFC 5545 folding and escaping), not a dependency. The invitation mailer is generalised into `apps/api/src/mailer/` with localised templates for all four locales (the existing one is English-only, survey section 12). In-app, the workspace and admin apps show a toast when a reminder lands while the user is online. Push and SMS are not in this epic.

**D-CW-27 (ruled). No chat in consultations; chat in workshops.** Consultations get only the quick signals "I cannot hear you", "I cannot see you" and the expert's "running late" message, delivered over Socket.IO and not persisted beyond the session (UX-14). Workshops get a session chat in its own table `call_messages` (session, participant, text, sent at), persisted until the session ends plus 24 hours for the facilitator's follow-up, never shown to the employer (UX-44); it is not stored in `call_events`, which holds provider events only.

**D-CW-28 (ruled). ### Decisions taken on the founder's answers (24 October 2026)

**D-CW-34 (settled by the founder, F-CW-01). Every webinar is recorded; consultations are not recorded for now.** The occurrence has no recording switch. Recording is announced at registration, in the confirmation email and in the lobby, starts with the webinar and stops with it (D-CW-18), and the replay always goes to the CMS review queue (D-CW-03). Consultations and workshops stay unrecorded (D-CW-19); the consent schema stays in place for a later decision.

**D-CW-35 (settled by the founder, F-CW-02). Attendees never have cameras; the floor is given by a host; attendees communicate through the chat.** Attendee capabilities are `Receive` on audio, video and content for the whole webinar. The webinar chat is one panel with two directions: hosts (presenters and the moderator) post to everyone; attendees write to the hosts, and their messages reach the audience only when a host publishes them, anonymous by default or with the first name the attendee attached. Questions are messages flagged as questions, so the moderator queue, approval, answering and dismissal of D-CW-16 apply to the same table (`webinar_messages`). An attendee who raises a hand can be given the floor by a host: audio becomes `SendReceive`, video stays `Receive`, the attendee unmutes to speak, and the host takes the floor back at any time (D-CW-15). Nothing an attendee does can turn on their camera, and the client has no control for it.

**D-CW-36 (ruled on F-CW-02). The audience ceiling is technical, not a product rule.** A single meeting carries 250 attendees (CORE-13); M3 adds media replication so an occurrence can take up to 1,000 (D-CW-14). Until M3 lands the occurrence form rejects a capacity above 250 with a message that names the limit and the milestone. The founder expects 200 to 300 attendees in the first year; the delivery model above 1,000 stays documented only.

**D-CW-37 (settled by the founder, F-CW-06). No extension; a follow-up is scheduled on the post-call screens, with manager approval when the client's policy says so.** The video room closes as in D-CW-09. When the expert ends the call, both participants land on post-call screens that stay open as long as they need, without the video running:

1. The expert's screen shows the outcome capture (D-CW-25) and, once the outcome is `completed`, a "Propose a follow-up" card: date, time (in the appointment's time zone, from a simple picker, since availability belongs to the booking epic), duration (same as this session by default), and a neutral note that the employee decides. Sending creates a new appointment with status `proposed`, `follow_up_of` set to the finished appointment, and emits `appointment.follow_up.proposed` on the `call:{sessionId}` room.
2. The employee's screen shows the feedback prompt (D-CW-25) and, when a proposal arrives, a "Your expert proposed a follow-up" card with "Confirm" and "Suggest another time" (the employee picks up to three time windows; the expert confirms one of them from the appointment page, which creates the confirmed appointment). The card also appears on the appointment page and in the appointment list, so a proposal made after the employee left the screen is not lost, and a reminder email "A follow-up was proposed" goes out with the neutral subject.
3. The organisation setting `follow_up_approval_policy` (`AUTOMATIC` by default, or `MANAGER_APPROVAL`) decides what confirmation does. `AUTOMATIC`: the appointment becomes `confirmed`, reminders are scheduled, the ICS is sent. `MANAGER_APPROVAL`: the appointment becomes `awaiting_approval` and an `appointment_approvals` row is routed to the holder of an approval authority that covers the employee's organisational unit (`approval.decide`, phase 2 approval authorities, with the self-approval rule and the alternate approver; the authority's `categories` are empty or contain `consultation_follow_up`); when the policy requires approval and no authority covers the employee's unit, the proposal fails visibly with `approver_missing`, the employee is told to ask HR to name an approver, and `appointment.approval.missing_approver` is audited; the approver sees the employee's name, "follow-up session with a NestSelf expert", the date and the duration, and never the service category, the expert's specialty or any content (F4, section 7 of the product flows). Approval confirms the appointment and notifies both parties; rejection sets `declined` with the approver's safe reason shown to the employee; no decision within 5 working days sends a reminder to the approver and the alternate. An undecided item lapses at the earlier of the proposed start and 10 working days after the request (amended 2026-10-25, T-CW-1.10): it becomes `expired`, the appointment is `declined` and the employee is told; a decision attempted after the start is refused with `approval_not_pending` and lapses the item.
4. The employee can decline a proposal (`declined`), and either party can cancel a confirmed follow-up as any appointment.

The booking epic later replaces the time picker with real availability and the approval item with its funding-aware approval, keeping these statuses and the approval table. Nothing here changes price or funding: the follow-up is an appointment like the first one, so the same payer rules apply once they exist.

**D-CW-37 addendum (25 October 2026, as implemented in T-CW-1.10).**

1. Lapse: an undecided approval item lapses at the earlier of the proposed start and 10 working days after the request. Working days are Monday to Friday in `Europe/Belgrade`; public holidays are not modelled (`apps/api/src/appointments/approval-schedule.ts`). The job `appointment.approval.expire` moves the item to `expired` and the appointment to `declined`, removes its jobs, audits `appointment.approval.expired` and notifies the employee (email `appointment.approval.decided`). The reminder after 5 working days is not scheduled when it would not come before the lapse, and because jobs are best effort a decision or a reminder after the start runs the same lapse first (the decision answers 409 `approval_not_pending`).
2. The request email (`appointment.approval.requested`) goes to the approver and to the alternate, like the reminder.
3. An authority with `isConfidentialApprover = true` is eligible. The flag adds a capability (its holder may also decide confidential items); it does not reserve the holder for confidential items, and a follow-up item carries no service category (LAW-14).
4. Self-approval: the guard checks `approval.decide` on the item's organisation, then the approval service calls `authorize()` a second time with the employee as `requesterId`, so the engine's self-approval step answers 403 `self_approval_denied` unless the organisation allows self-approval; the decider's authority must also still cover the employee's unit (404 otherwise). When the employee is the holder of a matching authority and self-approval is off, that authority is used only if it names a different alternate; the employee then neither sees the item nor receives its emails.

Consequences: the legal checklist (section 10) covers Serbian law and the GDPR-aligned ZZPL, and the Hungarian items from the research (LAW-19 to LAW-25) are kept as notes for a later Hungarian launch; the crisis block lists Serbian hotlines; the organisation default time zone stays `Europe/Belgrade`; replay captions start with Serbian; nothing in the four locales changes.

## 11. Known limitations of the implementation (25 October 2026)

The implementation on branch `feat/calls-p0` covers M0 to M2 and the replication of M3 (T-CW-3.2). These limitations are known and accepted for now; each has an owner in the follow-ups of `07-implementation-notes.md`.

1. **Service coordinators cannot moderate webinars.** The `service_coordinator` template holds `event.moderate`, `bookings.coordinate` and `appointment.propose` scoped to `assigned_queues`, and neither a webinar nor an appointment carries a queue, so the engine never matches. F-CW-07 named the service coordinator as a moderator; until the founder rules (a queue on the occurrence, an organisation-scoped coordinator grant, or dropping the coordinator from moderation), moderators are `client_operations` and `platform_owner`, and coordinators do not book or cancel consultations either.
2. **"Plan includes webinars" has no plan model.** D-CW-17 limits `all_eligible` to organisations whose plan includes webinars; no plan exists in the schema, so eligibility is the audience policy alone: `all_eligible` reaches every active member of every active client organisation.
3. **The time zone check accepts upper-case spellings.** `isIanaTimeZone` in `packages/contracts/src/appointments.ts` requires each segment to start with an upper-case letter or a digit and then asks `Intl`, which matches names case-insensitively, so `EUROPE/BELGRADE` is accepted and stored as typed; `europe/belgrade` is refused.
4. **The expert's photo and credentials have no data source.** `AppointmentExpertDisplaySchema` has optional `photoUrl` and `credentials` and the waiting state renders them when present, but the appointment repository fills neither, so the waiting state shows the expert's first name and initial only (CALL-05 criterion 1 in part).
5. **The replay handoff is a pending row only.** The finalize job writes `webinar_recordings.handoff_status = 'pending'` and `handoff_location` (the `final/{webinarId}/` folder); the one-line CMS adapter of D-CW-03 is added when the CMS media interface lands. The presenters' `webinar.recorded_notice` email has a template but is not sent yet.
6. **No spike has run against AWS.** SPIKE-CW-01 to SPIKE-CW-08 have scripts (`apps/api/scripts/spikes/`) and result templates (`research/07-spikes.md`); the decisions marked "spike" rest on the assumptions written there (for example that the capture pipeline takes one meeting seat, so a webinar meeting is created for its capacity plus one) until the founder runs them in the dev account.
7. **Hungarian hotlines are not included.** The crisis block lists Serbian hotlines only (D-CW-38); Hungarian numbers come with a Hungarian launch.
8. **A cancellation does not end a live meeting or send its email.** `POST /appointments/:id/cancel` moves the appointment to `canceled`, removes its jobs and audits it; no new join is accepted (the join checks the status), but participants already in the meeting stay until `calls.cleanup` deletes it at the room close, and the `appointment.canceled` email template exists without a sender (CALL-27 criteria 3 and 6).
