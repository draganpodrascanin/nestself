01 / overview

A human service. A precise system.

The public site should make people feel welcome. The product should make the next step, the cost and the boundaries of access easy to understand.

People bring the warmth

Real-life photographs, ordinary gestures and a voice that does not judge. No stock person is presented as a real expert or customer.

Clarity earns trust

Readable type, a visible next action, transparent coverage and honest statuses matter more than a colour’s supposed psychology.

One system, different density

The employee experience has room to breathe. HR and operations use denser data. Both share the same semantic tokens and component rules.

ContextWhat mattersDesign consequence

HR / managementManage a benefit and its budgetAggregate reporting; administrative eligibility stays separate from support history
Advisor / operationsManage assigned conversations and appointmentsExplicit owner, deadlines, status and permission boundaries
Public websiteUnderstand the service and the people it is forMore photography, clear service descriptions and verifiable claims

Selected direction: Forest & Mint + Onest + Phosphor. Four palettes and five sans families remain available in the controls. This HTML is the visual reference; the source package is the implementation handoff.

02 / colours

Change a palette. Keep the meaning.

All component colours come from CSS variables. A palette changes those variables once, across the homepage, the guide and every example.

Forest & Mint

Grounded green, fresh mint and a white base. People and photography carry the warmth. Brand green marks actions; success still needs a check and an explicit status.

Ink & Ice

Petrol and cool blue keep the interface precise. Useful when the HR and operational product should lead the identity.

Plum & Mist

Plum gives a different visual signature while white and cool grey keep the service open and practical. Avoid turning it into a luxury treatment brand.

Azure & Sun

A clearer blue structure with a small yellow accent. Informed by the broad blue-plus-warm-accent pattern in Modern Health and Headspace, with original NestSelf values and no cream canvas.

Weight, emphasis and numerals

400 for reading, 600 for orientation, 700 for a short important value. No weights below 400 in the app. Onest has no true italic here: use weight, quotation marks and layout. Enable lining tabular figures for numerical tables and align numbers right.

Reading, loading and language

Target 55–75 characters per prose line, max-width 65ch, with 16 px between paragraphs. Use self-hosted WOFF2, font-display: swap and a measured fallback. Test Serbian Latin and Cyrillic, long names and 30% longer strings. Native uses platform font scaling.

The public hero is a deliberate extension in v0.3: 64 / 70 at desktop scale instead of a slide-sized heading. App typography stays compact. Browser or native text scaling must increase height; do not clip text into fixed-height boxes.

The crop is a design decision

One image, three uses. The bad example deliberately loses a person. Set the focal point and review both wide and narrow layouts.

Rights and accessibility

Record source, photographer, licence and the appropriate approval scope. Informative alt describes the relevant context; decorative alt is empty; functional images name the action. Avoid repeating a visible person’s name unnecessarily. Never bake interface text into a photograph.

Delivery and CMS

Web: responsive WebP / AVIF with fallback, explicit width/height or aspect-ratio, and lazy loading below the first screen. The lead hero loads eagerly. Native formats and caching follow platform support. CMS: role, source, author, licence, approval, contextual alt, focal point and crop variants.

Validation and selection

Validate on leaving a field or submitting. A failed form focuses an error summary with links to the fields. Radio means one choice; checkbox means multiple choices or consent; switch means an immediate setting change. A placeholder never replaces a label.

Overlays and navigation

One modal task at a time. Trap focus in a modal, restore it on close and allow Escape when safe. Use a sheet for short mobile choices and a full screen for complex work. Toasts are not the only durable record of payment or confirmation.

Suppress small groups and combinations of filters that reveal individuals. Set the threshold and complementary suppression through an actual privacy risk assessment.

11 / resilience

The system works without ideal content.

Empty, loading, offline, stale, protected and denied states have different meanings. They should never collapse into a blank screen or an unexplained zero.

ConditionRequired response
Empty / no resultExplain what is absent and offer a relevant next step
LoadingUse the real layout; keep labels and dimensions stable
Offline / failureKeep the draft, expose retry and avoid duplicate action
Stale / occupied slotShow the last check and re-check before commitment
Permission / expired benefitExplain the limit and a safe return path
Protected aggregateState why it is unavailable; never display it as zero

Critical actions such as payment and booking do not show an optimistic success before the server confirms the final result. A toast is never the only record of the outcome.

One place to change the palette

The project setup brief

Pass the complete source package, then use this brief. It preserves the choices rather than asking the model to invent another design system.

Use this NestSelf Web Handoff as the design source of truth for the project setup.

1. Read README.md, CLAUDE.md, data/tokens.json and styles/tokens.css before building.
2. Preserve the selected default: Forest & Mint, light mode, Onest. Keep all four palettes and both modes available through semantic CSS variables.
3. Use the existing project stack. If this is a new project, set up a minimal maintainable frontend appropriate to the target; keep the design tokens framework-independent. Do not redesign the visual language while scaffolding.
4. Separate marketing, employee and HR surfaces. Keep the reference homepage image-led, with the same hierarchy, real-life photographs and responsive composition.
5. Build components from the documented contracts: buttons, fields, selections, feedback, navigation, dialogs, tables and the booking/coverage patterns. All colour in components must reference the semantic CSS variables. Keep state, size and density variants explicit.
6. Map the supplied local fonts to real weights 400, 600 and 700. Never synthesize italic. Use the original Phosphor SVG family, or its official framework package, with the documented weight rules.
7. Preserve the local image assets, credits and focal-point data. Use responsive production delivery. Stock people must not become named experts, customers or endorsements. Do not infer family eligibility from photographs.
8. Implement accessible keyboard, focus, form errors and announcements. Support reduced motion, 320 px reflow, text scaling, both language scripts and long content.
9. Treat the interactive examples as design demonstrations. Add no fake backend, payments, booked appointments, testimonials, client logos, availability promises or privacy guarantees.
10. Keep sample data visibly separate from production data. Enforce roles on the server when the service is connected; hiding UI is not access control.
11. Add a theme/token smoke check and meaningful component checks for keyboard, form errors and booking price clarity. Verify representative 390 px and 1440 px layouts in the actual browser.
12. Report the created structure, mapping of tokens, checks performed and any still-unimplemented states. Build the project setup first; do not broaden into an entire backend without an explicit brief.

Contract, not finished service: the examples are local. Authentication, availability, money movement, permissions, aggregate protection and the crisis pathway need real application and operational work. The final system review records these gaps explicitly.

How access should be separated

Your own support history, assigned professional access and the employer’s programme view have different purposes.

Employee: your own conversations and appointments.

Assigned advisor: the scope needed for your assigned case.

Employer: eligibility administration, programme budget and protected aggregate reporting, without individual support content.

Exceptional access: a defined, authorised and recorded operational process.

This is the proposed design model, not a live privacy notice or proof of implemented access control.
